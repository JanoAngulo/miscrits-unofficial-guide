Platinum Arena team guide

Four miscrits, twelve points. Build around one miscrit, cover its weakness, then chain the rest so each one covers the one before it.

Build a team

Random team rolls four that pass every check. Fill the gaps keeps your picks and fills the empty slots.

Rarity points

      Roles, attacks and defenses are read from base stats. Bonuses and relics move the real numbers, so treat them as a starting point. The address bar keeps this team, so you can bookmark or share it.

      How Platinum Arena works

      Platinum Arena is the game's main PvP format. A team is four miscrits worth 12 rarity points or fewer, and potions are banned.

      The cap is there to keep the meta varied. Two Legendaries cost 10 points, which leaves room for only two Commons beside them, so no small group of miscrits can dominate for good.

      Leaderboards

      Every arena has a leaderboard. The most points go to 16/16 teams: four relics on every miscrit, so nobody climbs by farming weaker teams. In Platinum Arena only, the top 200 when the arena resets on Monday get extra rewards. For picking those relics, see the relic and bonus guide.

      Accuracy

      Accuracy is pseudo-random, not a fresh roll each turn. All accuracy is rounded to the nearest 5%, so a 40% move with a 5% accuracy debuff stays at 40%: 38% rounds back up.

      Points by rarity

        Start with one miscrit

        Pick the miscrit you want to build around, because you like it or because it is strong in the current meta. Its speed decides its role, and its role decides who goes with it.

          Why speed decides the role

          At the start of a battle the faster miscrit moves first, and a tie is picked at random. From then on, the player with the slower miscrit holds speed control.

          • Sniping

            Faster than the enemy

            While you hold speed control, swapping in a miscrit faster than the enemy's gives you a double turn: you swap, then move again before they can. Most often that is a fast elemental attacker landing a strong hit. Speed control then passes to the other player, and you can't snipe again until they use theirs.

          • Slow tanks

            Slower than the enemy

            A tank is there to take the enemy's snipe. If it is slower than the enemy, speed control comes back to you and your sniper can go next. A tank faster than the enemy can't take it back, which is why players want red (low) speed on their tanks.

          • Status timing

            Poison, DoT and HoT

            DoT, HoT and Poison tick at the end of the turn of whoever holds speed control. Poison an enemy while they hold it and nothing happens until they move, so they can swap out first. A miscrit that relies on these needs speed control to get value from them.

          Swapping

          You can swap at any time unless you are Paralyzed. A swap costs your turn, and the miscrit you swap out moves to the end of your party.

          Swapping out clears most status effects. Bleed, Disease and Time-Bombs stay. Swap three times in a row without attacking and you take a damage penalty that keeps growing until you attack.

          Covering, and the two element cycles

          Most Platinum Arena teams play around one cycle. A miscrit covers a teammate when it beats the element that beats that teammate: if the enemy sends in a threat, the cover comes in and punishes it. An attack the target is weak to deals double damage, and one it resists deals half.

          Covering in practice

            Dual-element miscrits

            Simpler than it looks. A dual-element sniper just needs tanks and bruisers to cover, and maybe one miscrit to cover it back. It can often skip the cover and spend all three other slots on tanks and bruisers.

            A dual-element tank or bruiser usually needs a dual-element sniper sharing both elements, or one half covered by the sniper while the other half covers the sniper.

              Don't win only one kind of match

              A team that only works in certain match-ups loses the rest. A good Platinum Arena team has:

              • Both attacks

                Physical and elemental, so one kind of defense can't wall the whole team.

              • Both defenses

                Physical and elemental, so one kind of attack can't run through it.

              • Some utility

              Put them in order like a train

              Slowest first. Leading with your slowest miscrit makes you more likely to have the second turn, so you start with speed control.

              Then its cover. Second goes the miscrit that covers the first one's weakness, by element or by being strong where it is weak. Keep going the same way down the line.

              Never a Legendary starter. If it gets countered, you lose most of the value of its 5 points.
